Utterclip transcribes what you say on the device itself, rewrites it as the message you were about to type — to a colleague or to an assistant — and leaves it on your clipboard.

How it works
01 / CAPTURE

Tap the mic and talk. Stop talking and it notices. Said half of it? Continue picks up where you stopped and the rewrite re-runs on the whole thing.

02 / TRANSCRIBE
Whisper · on device

Transcribed on the phone by Whisper, then the audio is deleted. It never reaches a server.

03 / REWRITE
Slack WhatsApp Email Prompt Plain

Pick the shape it should take. It rewrites and copies. It never answers you — a question comes back as a question, not as a reply to it.

One recording, two pills.
Phone and Mac

One clipboard, whichever device you are holding.

ON THE MAC

It lives in the menu bar, in one small window that floats over whatever you are writing in. A global shortcut opens it beside the field you are typing in and starts recording.

HANDOFF

Dictate on the phone and the Mac window shows a capsule saying so, live. When the text has synced it reads New from iPhone. Click it and the dictation lands in the window and on the Mac clipboard.

PASTE BACK

A dictation started with the shortcut waits under the record button — Paste into Claude — and Return sends it into the field you came from.

Privacy

Your voice never leaves the device.

Audio is transcribed on the device and deleted straight after. It is never uploaded anywhere.

No account, no analytics, no tracking, and no server of ours.

Rewriting runs on the device, or through a provider with a key you supply — then only the transcript text and the style instruction go, with emails, phone numbers, links and addresses swapped for placeholders first.

Open source under MIT. Read exactly what it does, or build it yourself.
